Privacy Policy

Comment Lens | Version 0.3 | Last updated: 15 October 2026

Modes and availability: The public demo uses handwritten sample comments. Synthetic test previews also use fixed test data. Neither mode fetches the comments for a video you enter. A configured, connected build can offer account sign-in and backend analysis of public comments when its deployment and YouTube access are enabled. The dashboard identifies its current mode. The connected-service sections below apply when you use those features; their inclusion does not mean they are available in every build.

1. Who is responsible for your information

Siddhartha Ganguli develops and maintains Comment Lens as an independent personal project. For privacy questions, corrections, or deletion requests, email siddharthaganguli0093@gmail.com.

2. Information used in demo and test previews

When you open the extension popup, its active-tab permission lets it read that tab's title and URL to recognize a YouTube video. You can also paste a video link yourself. The selected link stays in the dashboard URL and may appear in your browser history. Demo and synthetic preview modes do not send it to a Comment Lens backend or to YouTube.

In these modes, charts, search, filters, and comment details use packaged sample data. These interactions happen in your browser. Demo mode has no account sign-in, advertising, analytics trackers, tracking cookies, or embedded YouTube player. A synthetic test account is simulated and does not authenticate with a provider. A CSV export is saved on your device when you choose Export CSV.

The public website and legal pages are hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub receives page requests and logs visitors' IP addresses for security, including visits without a GitHub account. Requested page URLs may contain a selected video link when you open or reload a dashboard URL that includes it. Hosting data is handled under the GitHub Privacy Statement. A local preview server may also log page requests.

3. Information used by a connected service

When live analysis is enabled, the connected service uses YouTube API Services, specifically YouTube Data API v3, to retrieve a limited sample of public information for the video you select:

  • Video information: video ID, title, channel name, publication time, and available comment count.
  • Comment information: public comment IDs, displayed text, author display names, likes, and publication times for up to 1,000 top-level comments.
  • Account and request information: an account email handled by Amazon Cognito, an internal account identifier, selected video ID, analysis request IDs, timestamps, and usage counters.
  • Analysis information: sentiment labels, model scores, processing status, sample coverage, and model version.

A connected website or extension signs in through Amazon Cognito. Cognito handles your account email and sign-in credentials; Comment Lens does not receive your password. This public-comment workflow does not use Google account authorization or request access to private YouTube information.

4. Why a connected service uses this information

Video and comment information supports the analysis you request: displaying the selected video, estimating sentiment in its sampled comments, and letting you inspect the original comment context. Account and request information controls access to your analysis history, applies usage limits, and helps handle errors or abuse.

Sentiment estimates are generated independently by Comment Lens. They describe a sample and can be wrong, especially for sarcasm or unfamiliar language. The serving workflow uses an existing model for prediction; it does not automatically add fetched comments to the model's training dataset. It is designed to analyze comment tone, without inferring sensitive personal characteristics about commenters.

5. Sharing and service providers

When live analysis is enabled, YouTube receives server-side API requests identifying the requested video. Google's handling of information is described in the Google Privacy Policy. YouTube use is also governed by the YouTube Terms of Service.

Connected deployments use Amazon Web Services for account authentication, processing, and private storage. The configured initial serving region is Northern Virginia, United States (us-east-1), so information may be processed outside your country. Amazon Cognito may set essential authentication cookies on its sign-in domain.

Several users requesting the same public video may receive the same cached public comments and predictions. Their account details and owned analysis history stay separate. A result-download link gives access to that result until the link expires; keep it private.

Privacy emails go to the contact's Gmail mailbox. Only the information needed to answer or verify a request should be included. Information may also be disclosed when required by a valid legal obligation. Comment Lens has no sale of personal information, advertising network, or data-broker integration.

6. Retention in a connected service

  • Analysis results and fetched comments: access lasts up to 24 hours from the original analysis creation. Cached results keep their original expiry and may expire sooner. Scheduled cleanup then removes stored objects, including old object versions. The configured storage cleanup backstop is seven days for missed or orphaned objects; physical deletion is separate from access expiry.
  • Request records: expired metadata is scheduled for removal after an additional one-hour cleanup window. Usage-accounting records have a separate short cleanup cycle.
  • Application logs: the configured retention is 14 days. Allowed diagnostic fields include request IDs, timing, counts, and error codes; comment text, author names, emails, tokens, and credentials are excluded.
  • Account information: it is retained until account closure and the related deletion are completed. Analysis expiry and signing out do not delete an account.

Downloaded CSV files stay on your device until you delete them. Privacy correspondence is kept only as long as needed to resolve the request or meet a specific legal obligation. GitHub controls its hosting logs under its Privacy Statement and does not specify a fixed GitHub Pages visitor-log duration there. These hosting logs are separate from the 14-day application logs of a connected deployment.

7. Your choices and deletion requests

Close the dashboard or remove the extension to stop its use. You can clear the selected video link from browser history and delete exported CSV files using your browser or device controls.

For information held by Comment Lens, email siddharthaganguli0093@gmail.com with the subject "Comment Lens privacy request." Explain whether you want access, correction, deletion, or an account closure. Include an analysis request ID or public video/comment link when it helps identify the information. Requests from people whose comments appear in a live analysis can use the same contact.

Reasonable identity checks may be needed to avoid deleting or revealing someone else's information. Valid deletion requests for stored user-related YouTube data will be handled as soon as possible and within seven calendar days of receipt, with any necessary verification handled promptly. Specific information may be kept where a legal obligation requires it; the reason will be explained.

In a connected service, removing an owned analysis request ends new access through that history entry. Shared public results may remain until their normal expiry. Previously issued download links may work for up to five minutes. To request removal of the underlying cached data or an account, use the privacy email; an automated account-deletion screen is not available.

Deleting information from Comment Lens does not remove the original video or comment from YouTube. Changes to that original content must be made through YouTube. Copies that users have already downloaded are outside Comment Lens's storage controls.

8. Security and browser storage

A connected backend uses encrypted connections, private cloud storage, ownership checks, limited service permissions, and expiring download links. YouTube API credentials stay on the backend and are not included in the website or extension. Demo and synthetic previews store no real login tokens.

Website sign-in: the website redirects you to Amazon Cognito and uses an authorization code with PKCE. A one-use sign-in state and verifier are kept in this tab's session storage. The attempt is valid for up to five minutes; its record is removed when the callback is processed or you sign out, and it is also cleared when the browser clears that tab's session data. After sign-in, the access token is held only in page memory. Reloading, leaving the page, or signing out clears that local token. The website does not persist ID or refresh tokens.

Extension sign-in: a connected Chrome extension keeps its access token in trusted Chrome session storage, rather than local or synced storage. It stops using an expired token and clears its stored session on sign-out; Chrome clears session storage when the browser session ends. The extension does not persist ID or refresh tokens.

Signing out of Comment Lens clears its local session. It does not close your account or necessarily clear Amazon Cognito's own sign-in cookies; those cookies may make a later sign-in quicker. You can manage provider cookies through your browser controls.

Security controls reduce risk but cannot guarantee that every transmission or device is secure. Privacy questions or suspected disclosures can be reported to the contact below.
